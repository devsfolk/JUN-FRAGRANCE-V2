If you live in Karachi, Lahore or Multan, you already know the problem. You spray perfume in the morning, and by the afternoon it has almost gone. Summer heat does that. The good news is that most of the fix is about how you apply and store perfume, not about spending more.

This guide is written for Pakistani summers: humid coastal days, dry and windy afternoons, crowded buses and long wedding evenings. Everything here is a habit you can start today.

## Why perfume fades faster in the heat

Perfume is a mix of fragrance oils and alcohol. Heat speeds up evaporation, so the lighter, brighter parts of a scent leave your skin sooner. Sweat and moving air carry the rest away. That is why a fresh citrus opening can vanish in the first hour of a hot day, while the same perfume feels steady in a cool room.

Humidity matters too. In coastal cities, damp air and sweat can make a heavy scent feel suffocating. In dry inland heat, a scent can seem to thin out quickly. In both cases, lighter application and a later top-up work better than a heavy first spray.

Our perfumes last approximately 6 to 8 hours. Treat that as a range, not a promise. Skin type, weather and the number of sprays all change the result, and in peak summer you should expect the lower end unless you follow the habits below.

## Start with the skin

### Apply after a shower

Clean skin gives the scent a fresh surface. If you spray over sweat, the smell mixes with it and fades unevenly. A shower, a towel dry and then perfume is the simplest improvement you can make.

### Use an unscented moisturiser

Dry skin lets fragrance disappear quickly. A thin layer of plain, unscented moisturiser or petroleum jelly on the spots where you spray gives the perfume something to hold on to. Avoid strongly scented lotions. They compete with the perfume and muddy it.

## Spray in the right places

### Pulse points and the chest

The inside of the wrists, the sides of the neck, the inside of the elbows and the upper chest are warm spots, so the scent opens up and travels with you. Hold the bottle about a hand's length from your skin and give it a quick, even spray.

### Do not rub your wrists together

It feels natural, but rubbing can dull the opening of a scent. Spray, then let it dry on its own.

### Hair and clothes, with care

A light mist on the ends of your hair holds scent well. Fabric also keeps scent longer than skin, but perfume can stain silk and light colours. Test on a hidden corner first, and keep your distance when you spray.

## How many sprays in summer

Two to four sprays are enough for most people. Piling on more does not make perfume last longer. It just makes the first hour heavy, especially in a hot rickshaw, a crowded wedding hall or an office with weak air conditioning. If you will be out all day, top up once before the evening instead of starting heavy in the morning.

A common mistake is spraying into the air and walking through the mist. Most of it lands on the floor, not on you. Spray directly on the skin instead.

## Pick scents that suit the heat

Cool, watery and citrus-led scents feel comfortable in hot weather, but they tend to fade sooner. Warmer woody, amber and oud-style scents often stay longer on skin, though they can feel thick at midday. You do not have to choose only one type.

For hot days, look at something with a cool, watery mood like [Deep Sea](/shop/deep-sea). For evenings and weddings, a deeper scent such as [Regal Oud](/shop/regal-oud) suits the cooler hours better. A daytime scent and an evening scent cover most of a week, and any three perfumes on the [bundle page](/bundle) cost Rs. 4,500.

## Store the bottle properly

Heat damages perfume slowly and quietly. A bottle left on a car dashboard, a sunny windowsill or a bathroom shelf will lose its freshness faster. Keep it in a cool, dry drawer or cupboard, with the cap on.

## A simple summer routine

1. Shower and dry your skin.
2. Add a thin layer of unscented moisturiser where you will spray.
3. Spray two to four times on the neck, chest and inside of the elbows.
4. Add a light mist to your hair or clothes, after testing on a hidden spot.
5. Top up once before an evening event instead of starting heavy.

Open any perfume page and you will see a list: bergamot, rose, vanilla, musk. It reads like a recipe, and for many people it means nothing. The idea behind it is actually simple, and once you get it, you can pick a perfume online with much more confidence.

## What perfume notes are

Notes are the individual scents you can pick out in a perfume, such as citrus, flowers, wood or vanilla. Perfumers arrange them in three layers, called top, heart and base. You do not smell all three at once. You smell them one after another as the perfume dries on your skin.

## Top notes: the first impression

Top notes are what you smell in the first few minutes after spraying. They are usually light and bright: citrus, fruit, fresh herbs. They are also the first to fade. Bergamot, which is a citrus fruit, and litchi are examples.

If you only sniff a perfume for ten seconds, top notes are all you are judging. That is why a quick sniff can mislead you.

## Heart notes: the character

Once the top notes calm down, the heart appears. This is the main character of the perfume, and it stays noticeable for a good part of the wear. Flowers such as rose, jasmine and peony often live here, along with spices and some woods. When someone calls a perfume floral, this is the layer they mean.

## Base notes: what remains

The base is the slow, deep layer that appears later and stays on your skin the longest. Woods, amber, musk, vanilla, patchouli and oud are typical base notes. When someone smells you after several hours and says you smell nice, they are usually smelling the base.

## How a perfume changes during the day

Think of it as three scenes. In the first few minutes, you get the opening: bright and sharp. Over the next while, the heart takes over and the scent feels rounder. Later, only the base is left, soft and close to the skin.

This is why two people can disagree about the same perfume. One judged it at the first sniff, the other after four hours. Heat also speeds up the whole sequence, which is why a perfume can feel different on a July afternoon than on a December evening.

## Common notes in plain words

### Citrus and fruit

Bergamot, lemon, orange, litchi, peach, passion fruit. Bright and juicy, sometimes sweet. They make a perfume feel awake.

### Flowers

Rose, jasmine, peony, lily of the valley. Soft, clean or powdery, depending on the flower. They are often the heart of a perfume.

### Woods, amber and musk

Sandalwood and other woods feel dry and warm. Amber is a warm, slightly sweet blend. Musk is a soft, skin-like note that makes a perfume feel close and comfortable.

### Vanilla and patchouli

Vanilla is sweet and creamy. Patchouli is earthy and, when used well, a little sweet. It is often paired with oud.

### Oud

Oud is a deep, resinous, woody note that many Pakistani buyers know from attar and incense. It is rich and strong, so a few sprays are enough.

Read that as a story: it opens juicy and bright, turns floral, and ends soft and creamy. That tells you it is a gentle, feminine scent and not a heavy evening one.

Compare it with [No.4 The Signature](/shop/no-4-the-signature), which lists rich bergamot, dark oud and sweet patchouli. It has the same three layers but a very different mood: deeper, smokier and more formal.

Judging by the first ten seconds is the first. Choosing by one favourite note without looking at the others is the second. Expecting the same result on every skin is the third. Perfume sits differently on each person, and heat changes the sequence.

seo_title: How to Store Perfume So It Does Not Spoil
seo_description: Heat, light and air spoil perfume. Simple storage rules for Pakistan's climate: where to keep your bottle and what to avoid.
--- content ---
A perfume bottle can look fine for months and still lose its freshness. Heat, light and air change a scent slowly, and by the time you notice, the top notes may already have turned dull or sharp. In Pakistan, where summers are long and power cuts can heat a room fast, storage matters more than most people think.

The rules are simple. Here is what works.

## What spoils perfume

Four things do the damage: heat, direct sunlight, air and humidity. Heat speeds up the changes inside the bottle. Sunlight can alter the colour and the smell. Air gets in each time you open the bottle, and a cap left loose makes it worse. Damp rooms are not ideal either.

Sudden changes also matter. A bottle that goes from a cold room to a hot car and back again has a harder life than one that stays in a steady, cool spot.

## Where to keep your perfume

Choose a cool, dry, dark place. A drawer in your bedroom is a good choice. So is a closed cupboard or a wardrobe shelf, away from windows and radiators or heaters.

In summer, pick the coolest room in the house, and not the one that heats up first when the power goes. If you can, keep perfume away from the outer wall that takes the afternoon sun.

## Where not to keep it

Avoid the bathroom. Showers raise the heat and humidity, and the shelf above the sink gets steamy. Avoid the car dashboard, the glove box and the boot, where heat builds up quickly. Avoid sunny windowsills and the tops of cupboards near a window. Avoid the kitchen too, because cooking heat lingers there.

A bike box or a bag left in the sun is also a poor home for a bottle.

## Keep the cap on

Close the bottle after every use. It sounds obvious, but a loose cap lets air in and lets the scent escape. If your bottle has an outer cap or sleeve, snap it shut each time.

## Use the box

If you have the box, use it. A box keeps out light and gives some protection from temperature swings. It is especially helpful if you keep a bottle in a drawer that opens often.

## Travelling with perfume

Carry the bottle in a bag you keep with you, not in a car or a luggage hold where it can sit in the heat. Wrap it in a soft cloth or a sock so it does not knock against other things. At your destination, put it in a cool drawer as soon as you arrive.

## Signs a perfume has turned

Look for a darker colour than before, a sharp or sour smell that was not there at the start, or a scent that smells flat with the fresh top notes gone. These can be signs of heat or age, not proof. If you are unsure, test a small spray on skin and compare it to what you remember.

## A quick storage checklist

1. Keep it cool, dry and out of the sun.
2. Choose a drawer or closed cupboard in the coolest room.
3. Keep it out of the bathroom, the car and the kitchen.
4. Close the cap after every use.
5. Keep the box if you can.
6. Do not leave it in a bag in the heat.

## Use it, do not hoard it

A perfume you love is meant to be worn. Good storage helps, but there is little point in keeping a bottle untouched for years. If you own several scents, rotating them is a good habit. Our [bundle page](/bundle) lets you pick any three perfumes for Rs. 4,500, which is an easy way to build a small rotation. You can also browse the [shop](/shop) to find a scent for the next season.
